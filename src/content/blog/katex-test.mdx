This post tests **KaTeX** math rendering in MDX. Both inline and display math should render correctly.

## Inline math

Newton's second law: $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$. Mass-energy equivalence: $E = mc^2$.

Specific impulse: $I_{sp} = \frac{v_e}{g_0}$, where $v_e$ is exhaust velocity and $g_0 = 9.80665\ \text{m/s}^2$.

A quadratic formula: $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.

## Display math

### Tsiolkovsky rocket equation

$$
\Delta v = v_e \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) = I_{sp}\ g_0 \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right)
$$

Where $\Delta v$ is delta-v, $m_0$ initial mass, $m_f$ final mass.

### Orbital period (Kepler's third law)

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}}
$$

For a circular orbit: $T = \frac{2\pi r^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\mu}}$, where $\mu = GM$ is the standard gravitational parameter.

### Specific orbital energy

$$
\varepsilon = \frac{v^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r} = -\frac{\mu}{2a}
$$

The vis-viva equation:

$$
v = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right)}
$$

### Matrices and vectors

State vector in Cartesian coordinates:

$$
\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \\ \dot{z} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Rotation matrix about the $z$-axis:

$$
R_z(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\
\sin\theta &  \cos\theta & 0 \\
0          &  0          & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Integrals

Gravitational potential of a continuous mass distribution:

$$
U(\mathbf{r}) = -G \iiint_V \frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}')}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|}\ d^3\mathbf{r}'
$$

### Maxwell's equations (differential form)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} &= \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0} \\[4pt]
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} &= 0 \\[4pt]
\nabla \times \mathbf{E} &= -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} \\[4pt]
\nabla \times \mathbf{B} &= \mu_0\mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Inline with display context

The Hohmann transfer delta-v from orbit 1 to orbit 2:

$$
\Delta v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_1}} \left( \sqrt{\frac{2r_2}{r_1 + r_2}} - 1 \right)
$$

$$
\Delta v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_2}} \left( 1 - \sqrt{\frac{2r_1}{r_1 + r_2}} \right)
$$

Total: $\Delta v_\text{total} = |\Delta v_1| + |\Delta v_2|$.
